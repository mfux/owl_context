# Image Generation Prompt — Day 1, Image 2

*12 March 2026 — Arc 1: First Arrival, Strip 4 / Arc 3: The Windowsill*

## Scene

A small, fluffy young owl — Konsti — sits on the wide windowsill of the Bauwagen, bathed in the warm, low light of late afternoon. This is not arrival anymore. This is belonging.

The view is from outside, looking slightly upward at the Bauwagen's large glass window — the same angle as the previous image, but everything has changed. Hours have passed. Konsti is lower on the sill now, his body settled deep into his own feathers, his weight dropped, his whole posture saying *I am exactly where I should be.* He sits in a pool of direct sunlight that falls across the sill from the west-southwest — a warm diagonal band of light that makes his fluffy feathers glow in bright highlights and casts a soft shadow behind him against the glass.

His eyes are half-closed. Not sleepy — content. The particular half-lidded look of a creature who has found a warm spot and sees no reason to leave it. His ear tufts are relaxed, neither up nor flat — at their natural resting angle. His talons are loose on the sill, no longer gripping with the deliberation of a first landing. He looks like he has been here forever.

## Environment

The Schrottplatz in the late afternoon of a March day that turned unexpectedly beautiful. The sky behind and above is mostly clear — vast and pale, fading from a bright western horizon toward a cooler, slightly deeper tone overhead. Only a few wisps of cloud remain, caught by the low sun.

The low-angle sunlight transforms the scrapyard. Every surface is side-lit: the rusted metal of the junk piles catches bright highlights on their west-facing edges while their east sides fall into deep shadow. Long shadows stretch across the yard from every vertical object — bare tree trunks, fence posts, stacked pallets — all pointing roughly northeast, creating a pattern of bright and dark stripes across the ground.

The Bauwagen window behind Konsti is clearer now than in the first image. The condensation that fogged the lower edges this afternoon has mostly evaporated in the warm air — only a faint trace remains in the bottom corners. Through the glass, the dim interior is more visible: dark shelves, a table edge, a hanging tool silhouette. The glass itself reflects the clear sky and the golden light.

The windowsill is worn wood, now dry — no longer beaded with moisture from the morning. Its grain catches the directional light, every crack and weathered mark visible. The warmth of the wood almost feels palpable.

Near the base of the Bauwagen, the cluster of broken terracotta flower pots. The hornveilchen shoots are catching the last direct light — small bright-toned points among the darker pot fragments. They have the determined quality of things that know what they're doing.

The bare trees in the background are sharply silhouetted — dark skeletal branches against the bright western sky. One evergreen (the same spruce from the first image) provides a mass of deep dark tone in the mid-left background. The ground has dried through the afternoon; the puddles have shrunk to small bright mirrors in the lowest spots, reflecting the sky.

Further back, half-hidden by the scrap piles, the peaked roof of the toolshed is visible. Its door is still closed, still slightly ajar.

## Background Stories

- On the window frame, above and to the right of Konsti, the spider's web is nearly complete. In the first image it was half-built — now only one spoke is unfinished. The spider herself is visible at the hub, tiny, dark, and patient. She has had a productive afternoon.
- The loose sheet of corrugated metal that was vibrating in the wind during the first image is now still and quiet — the wind has eased. It leans at the same angle but everything about it says *settled.*
- Near the hornveilchen, a single small beetle has appeared on the rim of a broken flower pot — the first insect of the season, drawn out by the unusual warmth of the afternoon. It is very small but visible, its dark form sharp against the sun-lit terracotta.
- The crow that was on the distant scrap pile in the first image is gone. The pile is empty. Where did it go? A small mystery.
- In the far background, barely visible, a cat's silhouette sits on a fence post — another creature enjoying the unexpected warmth. Just a shape. Just a presence.

## Mood

Deep contentment. The golden warmth of a late afternoon that turned out better than anyone expected. This is the feeling of a first day becoming home — not the excitement of discovery (that was the first image), but the quiet satisfaction of staying. The kind of warmth that makes you close your eyes halfway and just *be.*

The atmosphere is gentle, safe, and full of the particular beauty of low sunlight in grayscale — dramatic contrasts, long shadows, bright highlights that do the work of golden color. The world is winding down toward evening, and everything on the Schrottplatz has the peaceful, unhurried quality of a day that gave more than it promised.

## Art Style & Technical Requirements

- High-resolution pixel art in Studio Ghibli style — "256-bit, not 8-bit." Every pixel is deliberate. The image is dense with detail and atmosphere. The worn wood of the windowsill, the fine individual feathers of Konsti's fluffy body catching the directional light, the delicate strands of the spider's web, the texture of rusted metal — all rendered in meticulous pixel detail.
- **Grayscale only** — the full range of 256 shades from deep black to clean white. No color whatsoever. The late-afternoon directional light is the star here: use strong contrast between lit and shadowed surfaces. Bright, almost-white highlights where the sun hits (Konsti's feathers, the windowsill, wet spots reflecting sky). Deep blacks in the shadows (behind/below the Bauwagen, the window interior, the east sides of junk piles). Rich midtones for everything between.
- **800×600 pixels, landscape orientation (4:3).** Image fills the frame edge to edge. No borders, no frame.
- **No text, no speech bubbles, no captions, no letters.** Not a single character of text anywhere in the image.
- **No accessories on Konsti.** He is just himself — a small, fluffy, round young owl.
- **Rich environmental detail always.** Every corner of the Schrottplatz is alive with texture and story.
- **Konsti is the emotional center and focal point** — positioned in the lower-center of the composition on the windowsill, lit by the warm diagonal of afternoon sun, with the large window and the yard framing him. The eye should go to him first and find contentment there before wandering into the background details.
